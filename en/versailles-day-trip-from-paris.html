Versailles, as a day trip

If your trip is longer than the usual quick pass through Paris, Versailles is the one day trip that's actually worth the hour on the train. Here's what it costs, how to get there without a tour group, and which day to skip.

Is it worth a full day?

If you're on a tight multi-city itinerary, Versailles is the first thing that gets cut, and that's a reasonable call — the round trip alone eats two hours. But if you have four or more days in Paris (increasingly the norm for European, Australian, and UK honeymooners, whose trips tend to run 10-14 days against the roughly 7-8 day average for US couples), it's a genuinely different experience from anything inside the city: the scale of the Hall of Mirrors and the gardens doesn't come across in photos, and nothing in Paris itself is comparable. Budget a full day rather than a rushed half-day — between the Palace, the gardens, and the Estate of Trianon, there's more to see than a 3-hour visit covers.

Getting there

RER C to Versailles Château Rive Gauche

This is the standard route: RER C, direction Versailles Château Rive Gauche, which is a 5-minute walk from the Palace entrance. The line passes through several central stations on the way — Gare d'Austerlitz, Saint-Michel Notre-Dame, Musée d'Orsay, Invalides, Pont de l'Alma, Champ de Mars/Tour Eiffel — so depending on where you're staying, you can often board without changing lines. Journey time is roughly 40-50 minutes each way; a round-trip ticket runs about €5-7, separate from Palace admission. Double-check the train's final destination before boarding — RER C splits into several branches, and only trains signed for Versailles Château Rive Gauche go where you want.

Tickets

Passport ticket (Palace + Trianon + gardens) — what most visitors want

€25 in low season (November-March), €35 in high season (April-October). This covers timed entry to the Palace, the Estate of Trianon (Grand Trianon, Petit Trianon, the Queen's Hamlet), and the gardens.

Estate of Trianon only

€15, year-round, if you specifically want the Trianon estate without the main Palace — a smaller, quieter part of the grounds that's easy to skip by accident if you don't know it's there.

Timed entry is mandatory now

Even visitors with free admission (under-18s, EU/EEA residents under 26) need to reserve a free timed slot online in advance — you can't just show up. Book directly through the official site to avoid third-party markup.

Best day and time to go

The Palace is closed every Monday — the same day as the Musée d'Orsay in Paris, so plan your week accordingly (see the opening hours guide for the rest of the city). Tuesdays, Saturdays, and Sundays tend to be the busiest of the remaining days. If your schedule allows it, a Wednesday, Thursday, or Friday with an early time slot — the Palace opens at 9am — is the most manageable combination. The gardens themselves are open daily, Monday included, if you only want to walk the grounds.

Want the palace to yourself? You can actually stay there

There's exactly one hotel inside the grounds of Versailles — Airelles Château de Versailles, Le Grand Contrôle, a 17th-century residence built by the Palace's own architect, with just 14 rooms. What makes it worth mentioning here specifically: guests get a private tour of the Palace, Hall of Mirrors included, before or after normal visiting hours, with no other visitors on the grounds — something no day-trip ticket, at any price, can buy. Garden access by golf cart and a boat ride on the Grand Canal are also part of a stay. It's genuinely expensive and it's not a convenient Paris base for the rest of your trip, but as a once-in-a-trip splurge for a honeymoon, it's a different category of experience from a day visit.
