Paris airport transfers

CDG, Orly, and Beauvais compared by train, bus, and taxi/rideshare, with real fares and journey times. Discontinued services (the old Roissybus, the old Orlybus) aren't listed — they no longer run.

CDG (Charles de Gaulle) Orly Beauvais

CDG (Charles de Gaulle Airport)

About 25km northeast of central Paris, with Terminals 1, 2, and 3. RER B has two separate stations — "Aéroport CDG 1" (for T1/T3) and "Aéroport CDG 2" (for T2 and the TGV station) — so confirm your terminal before you board. Quick guidance: light luggage and daytime arrival → RER B; late-night/early-morning arrival → the Noctilien night bus; more people or luggage → a rideshare (VTC). A direct express rail link, "CDG Express," is under construction but not expected to open until March 2027, so it isn't an option yet in 2026.

RER B (train)

Journey time: about 35 min to Gare du Nord, about 35 min to Châtelet-Les Halles

The standard public transit option, running roughly 5:00-24:00 every 6-15 minutes. The fare is €14 one-way (€7 reduced rate, since a January 2025 update) on the dedicated "Titre Paris Région ⟷ Aéroports" ticket — a Navigo weekly/monthly/annual pass (zones 1-5) or a Paris Visite pass also covers it. The old direct shuttle bus, "Roissybus," was discontinued at the end of February 2026; RER B is now the official recommended public-transit alternative.

Noctilien (night bus)

Journey time: about 80 min on N140, about 55 min on N143 (both end at Gare de l'Est)

When RER B isn't running (roughly 0:30-5:00), the N140 and N143 night buses run year-round every 30-60 minutes. Fare is one standard bus/tram ticket (€2). This is your public-transit option for a genuine late-night or early-morning arrival.

Rideshare (Uber etc.) — recommended over a street taxi

Worth booking ahead of a taxi for the reason explained below. Pricing is surge-based, but the app shows a confirmed fare before you accept, so there's no ambiguity about what you'll pay when you land. Peak periods can push the price up.

Taxi (fixed rate — but read the warning below)

Paris ⟷ CDG: €56 on the Right Bank (rive droite) · €65 on the Left Bank (rive gauche)

Set by a French government decree dated December 24, 2025 (effective 2026), this flat rate should apply regardless of traffic or luggage, for up to 4 passengers, for any pickup/drop-off within Paris itself (trips starting or ending in the suburbs use the meter or a separate quote). A 20% surcharge applies at night (22:00-6:00), Sundays, and public holidays.
Read this before you land: despite the fixed rate being law, there are reported cases of unofficial touts approaching arriving passengers, and of some drivers falsely claiming the flat rate "doesn't apply" to overcharge. Only use the official taxi rank (look for the marked queue), confirm before getting in with "C'est bien le forfait ?" (seh byan luh for-FEH — "is this the flat rate?"), and always get a receipt.

Arrêté du 24 décembre 2025 (Légifrance, official) ↗

If you do get overcharged

There's no reliable way to get an immediate refund by arguing it out on the spot, and trying usually just costs you time. On the spot: note the vehicle's plate number and the driver's ID number (legally required to be displayed in the back seat), keep the receipt, and pay by card if at all possible — a card payment is much easier to dispute later than cash. After you're home: (1) if you paid by card, ask your card issuer about a chargeback for an unauthorized/incorrect charge, (2) report it (free, can be anonymous) to France's consumer fraud portal, SignalConso, with the driver/vehicle details and evidence, and (3) the Paris police prefecture has a dedicated complaint form for taxi/rideshare drivers. None of these guarantee a refund, but they do feed enforcement against repeat offenders.

Orly Airport

About 13km south of central Paris — closer in than CDG. Metro line 14 was extended to Orly in June 2024 and is now the main public-transit route; the old direct "Orlybus" shuttle was discontinued in March 2025. Quick guidance: light luggage and daytime → Metro 14; coming from the southeast (13th/14th arrondissement area) → Tram T7; late-night/early-morning → the Noctilien night bus.

Metro Line 14

Journey time: about 25 min to Châtelet

A driverless metro extension opened June 24, 2024, now the main public-transit route to Orly. Fare is a standard metro ticket (€2.55 as of 2026). No transfer needed to reach the city center, a real improvement over the old Orlybus/Orlyval-only routing.

RATP (official) ↗

Orlyval + RER B (via Antony)

Journey time: about 8 min shuttle + RER B onward

The automated Orlyval shuttle to RER B's Antony station still runs alongside Metro 14, and can be more convenient if you're headed somewhere along the southern RER B corridor. During RER B engineering works in summer 2026 (planned 7/25-8/16), Navigo/Imagine R pass holders get free Orlyval rides as a temporary measure — dates can shift, so check before relying on it.

Tram T7

Journey time: about 30 min to Villejuif-Louis Aragon (connects to Metro 7)

Useful if you're headed to the southeast, along the Metro 7 corridor. Same fare as a standard metro/bus ticket (€2.55).

Noctilien (night bus)

Lines N22, N31, N131, and N144 cover late-night/early-morning arrivals. Standard bus/tram fare.

Rideshare (Uber etc.) — recommended over a street taxi

Same reasoning as CDG above (see the taxi warning there — it applies equally at Orly). Surge pricing, but with a confirmed fare shown before you accept.

Taxi (fixed rate — same warning as CDG)

Paris ⟷ Orly: €45 on the Right Bank (rive droite) · €36 on the Left Bank (rive gauche)

Set by the same December 24, 2025 decree as CDG. Because Orly is south of the city, the Left Bank rate is cheaper than the Right Bank here — the opposite of CDG. Same 20% night/Sunday/holiday surcharge, and the same overcharging risk — use the official taxi rank, confirm the flat rate before you get in, and keep your receipt.

Beauvais Airport (BVA)

About 85km north of Paris — much farther out, mainly served by budget/low-cost airlines. There's no direct public transit line; the official shuttle bus is the standard option. Build in extra time relative to your flight given the distance.

Official shuttle, "Navette Aérobus" (recommended)

Journey time: from about 1h15 (traffic-dependent) · Fare: €17.90 one-way / €29.90 round-trip online (in-person purchase costs more, and on-site payment is card-only)

Runs directly between Paris Porte Maillot (the "Pershing" bus terminal) and the airport. Booking ahead is required as of 2026. Departs Paris 3:00-19:30 every 15-30 minutes; the recommendation is to catch the bus 3h30 before your flight. On arrival, a bus is timed to depart roughly 20 minutes after landing. Online booking stays open right up to departure.

Aéroport Paris-Beauvais (official) ↗

Budget route: train + local bus

From Paris Gare du Nord, an SNCF TER regional train to Beauvais station takes about 1h15 (roughly €10+), then local bus "Corolis" line 6 covers the last stretch to the airport in about 20 minutes (€1-2). Can be cheaper than the official shuttle, but involves a transfer and less frequent service — not the move if your flight timing is tight.

Taxi

At 85km, a taxi from central Paris is expensive — figure €150-200+. Only worth considering with a larger group splitting the cost.
