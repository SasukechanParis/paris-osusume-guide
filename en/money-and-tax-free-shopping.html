Money & tax-free shopping

Two separate topics that both save real money: avoiding your card's foreign transaction markup, and correctly claiming back VAT on eligible purchases.

Avoiding card fees Tax-free shopping (détaxe)

Avoiding foreign transaction fees

A typical US or UK bank card adds a foreign transaction markup on top of the exchange rate — often 1.6-3% per purchase, which adds up fast on a week-long trip. Wise and Revolut are travel-focused accounts that both let you set up before you leave, from your home country, and cut that markup down to close to nothing.

Wise

Converts at close to the real mid-market exchange rate, with no weekend markup, and free ATM withdrawals up to a set monthly amount. Set up: download the app, open an account from home, transfer money in from your regular bank account, convert to euros in-app, then spend on the Wise card (Apple Pay/Google Pay compatible) or withdraw from an ATM.

Revolut

Similar idea — fee-free conversion up to a monthly limit that depends on your plan, and you can hold several currencies in the app at once. The standard plan does add a small weekend exchange markup (around 1%), which is the main practical difference from Wise. Set up is the same pattern: download, verify by phone/SMS, fund from your bank or a service like PayPal, convert, and spend.

Tax-free shopping (détaxe / PABLO)

If you live outside the EU, you can reclaim part of the French VAT (TVA) on eligible purchases when you leave. This is the current process per French customs (douane.gouv.fr) and the Ministry of the Economy, as of 2026.

Who qualifies

You need to live outside the EU, be 16 or older, and have stayed in France under 6 months. Purchases must total over €100.01 at a single store on a single day, and must be new, unused goods — restaurant meals, hotel stays, taxis, and services in general don't qualify, and neither do tobacco, weapons, cultural property, fuel, or private vehicles.

At the store

Ask at checkout: "Je voudrais la détaxe, s'il vous plaît" (zhuh voo-DREH lah day-TAKS, seel voo PLEH — "I'd like the tax refund, please") and show your passport. The store issues an electronic refund form; give them your email so you get a confirmation. The refund isn't the full VAT — the store deducts a processing fee (roughly 3-5%), so the real refund works out to around 12% of the purchase price at most.

The step people forget: the PABLO terminal, before you check your bag

On your departure day, before you check your luggage, you need to scan your refund form's barcode at a PABLO self-service terminal (French customs' validation kiosk) at the airport. This is the single most common mistake: if you check your bag first and the item is inside it, customs can't verify the goods if asked, and you may not be able to complete validation. Keep détaxe items in your carry-on, or validate at PABLO before you check in. If you're connecting through another EU country on the way home, you validate at the last EU airport you leave from, not in France. If the terminal won't scan your form, go to the customs desk nearby rather than giving up.

Getting the refund

After PABLO validates your form, the refund lands however the store set it up — usually back to your card (can take a few weeks) or, less commonly, in cash at an airport refund counter. If validation fails, no refund is issued — which is exactly why the timing above matters.
