Concours de Boulangerie · Paris

Paris's real bakery competitions

Every "best croissant in Paris" list you'll find online is somebody's opinion. These two aren't. The City of Paris and the local bakers' guild each run a judged competition, once a year, with a jury and an actual winner — and most of that record isn't published anywhere in English. Here's how the two competitions work, and who's won them going back over a decade.

How these competitions actually work

Grand Prix de la Meilleure Baguette de Paris

Run by the City of Paris every year since 1994. Around 200 bakeries enter, a jury tastes the baguettes blind, and each one is scored on five criteria — bake, crumb and holes, smell, taste, and appearance. The winner gets a medal, €4,000, and one more thing that makes this genuinely more interesting than a normal food award: for the following year, their bakery becomes the official baguette supplier to the Élysée Palace, feeding the French President's household. That part is real and documented, not a rumor — see the source below.

Meilleur Croissant au Beurre du Grand Paris

Run annually by the Syndicat des Boulangers du Grand Paris — the bakers' guild covering Paris and the three neighboring départements (Hauts-de-Seine, Seine-Saint-Denis, Val-de-Marne). Every entry has to be made with Charentes-Poitou AOP butter, and the jury judges on appearance, lamination, texture, aroma, and taste. Results are usually announced in spring at the Fête du Pain.

Sources: Concours de la meilleure baguette de Paris (Wikipedia) · Syndicat des Boulangers du Grand Paris
