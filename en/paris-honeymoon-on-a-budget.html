Paris on a budget, and what's worth the splurge

Most honeymoons aren't purely budget or purely luxury — they're careful spending most days, and one or two deliberate splurges. Here's where Paris genuinely lets you save, and where the extra money is actually well spent rather than just spent.

Where to actually save

The biggest, easiest savings aren't about cutting experiences — they're logistics most visitors just don't know about. A Wise or Revolut card avoids the 1.6-3% foreign transaction markup a typical home bank card charges on every purchase (see the money guide); the metro is a few euros a ride against a taxi's flat-rate fares that add up fast over a week; and if you're buying anything substantial (jewelry, designer goods), the détaxe tax refund genuinely returns real money if you do the PABLO step correctly. None of this requires giving anything up — it's just not leaving money on the table.

Free and cheap things to do

Free museums, permanent collections, year-round

Paris's municipal museums are free every day for their permanent collections (temporary exhibitions inside them usually aren't). Three worth a couple's afternoon: Petit Palais — a Belle Époque building with a genuinely pretty interior courtyard, easy to combine with the free Pont des Arts view nearby; Musée Carnavalet — the history of Paris itself, recently and well renovated; and Musée Cognacq-Jay — a compact 18th-century townhouse full of period furniture, small enough to see properly in under an hour. None need advance booking for the permanent collection.

Everything already free in the honeymoon guide

Worth remembering: most of what's already in the romantic things to do and walks sections costs nothing — the Eiffel Tower's evening sparkle from Trocadéro, the free rooftop at Printemps, Pont des Arts, the Île de la Cité to Île Saint-Louis walk, the Canal Saint-Martin walk. A genuinely romantic day in Paris doesn't require a ticket for most of it.

Eating well without a big bill

Picnic like a local

A bakery for bread, a fromagerie for a wedge of cheese, and a wine shop or supermarket for a bottle — a decent French supermarket bottle in the €5-7 range holds its own against wine costing three times as much on a restaurant list. Eaten on the banks of the Seine, in the Luxembourg Gardens, or along Canal Saint-Martin, this is a genuinely nice meal for well under €35 total, not a consolation prize.

Bouillon Chartier — a €20 meal in a room that hasn't changed since 1896

A "bouillon" is a specific, historic style of Parisian restaurant built around exactly this idea: a proper meal, served fast, at a price that was never meant to be precious about it. Chartier's original Belle Époque dining room near the Grands Boulevards is still intact — it's as much the reason to go as the food. Mains start around €7, a full meal comes in under €20, and no reservation is needed (there can be a short wait at peak times).

Hotels: our honest take

This guide's hotel picks are chosen for location first, which tends to skew toward mid-range and above — a genuinely central, walkable location in Paris rarely comes at a rock-bottom price. Two on the list are flagged as relative value picks (Hôtel Thérèse near the Louvre, Hôtel Duquesne Eiffel near the Champ de Mars) — well-located without the price tag of the palace hotels nearby. If budget is the priority over location, that's a real trade-off worth going in aware of, not something we'll pretend doesn't exist.

Where to splurge instead

If the budget only stretches to one or two big moments, these are the ones that actually deliver something a cheaper version can't: a table at one of Paris's Michelin-starred restaurants rather than a mid-tier "fine dining" experience with none of the craft behind it; a single night at a genuine destination hotel — Airelles Château de Versailles for private after-hours palace access, or Molitor for its restored Art Deco pool — even if the rest of the trip is at a more modest hotel; or a proper sunset Seine cruise timed for golden light (see the romantic things to do section), rather than a daytime version at a third of the price that misses the entire point of doing it at all.
