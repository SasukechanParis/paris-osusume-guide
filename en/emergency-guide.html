Situations

My phone was stolen or lost

① Call your carrier's international lost/stolen line to suspend your number
② Remotely lock/erase it with Apple "Find My" or Google's "Find My Device"
③ File a police report (link below)

Pré-plainte en ligne (pre-report online) ↗
More detail

Save your carrier's international lost/stolen hotline before you travel — you'll need a way to reach it without the phone that's now missing. If you back up photos and contacts before your trip, losing the device stops being a data disaster and becomes just an inconvenience. For a non-urgent police report in France, the online "pré-plainte en ligne" lets you book a time slot at your nearest police station (commissariat) so you're not stuck waiting in person.

My wallet was stolen

① Cash inside is essentially unrecoverable
② Cancel every card that was in it
③ File a police report anyway — you may need it for insurance

Pré-plainte en ligne (pre-report online) ↗
More detail

See "my credit card was stolen" below for cancelling cards. If you have travel insurance, the police report's reference number (récépissé) is often required to file a claim — worth the trip to the station even though nothing about a stolen wallet is fun.

My credit card was stolen

① Call the number on the back of the card, or freeze it in your bank's app, immediately
② France also has a shared emergency line covering most French-issued cards

0892 705 705 (shared French bank card block line)
More detail

Calling your own card issuer directly (the number on the card, or your banking app) is usually the more reliable option. 0892 705 705 is a shared line run jointly by French banks — it's a paid number (around €0.35/min) but works for cancelling any French-issued card. Whichever route you use, keep the cancellation reference number (numéro d'opposition) they give you, and file a police report if the card was used fraudulently.

I lost my passport

① Report it at your nearest police station (commissariat) and get the paperwork
② Contact your embassy

More detail

Get the police report first — your embassy will want it. Then contact your country's embassy or consulate in Paris to start an emergency passport or replacement process; requirements and processing time vary by nationality, so check your own embassy's current guidance rather than assuming a fixed timeline. If your flight home is imminent, ask specifically about an emergency travel document, which is usually faster than a full passport replacement.

I'm feeling unwell

Minor symptoms → a pharmacy (green cross sign) can advise
Severe symptoms or a high fever that won't break → call 15 (SAMU)

15 (SAMU / emergency medical)
More detail

French pharmacies (pharmacie, marked by a green cross) are genuinely useful for minor issues — pharmacists can recommend over-the-counter treatment for common ailments. If it's outside normal hours, see "I need a pharmacy at night or on a holiday" below. If you have travel insurance, their emergency line can often direct you to a partner hospital or clinic.

I'm hurt

Minor injury → a pharmacy can help with first-aid supplies
Heavy bleeding, suspected fracture, etc. → call 15 (SAMU) or 18 (fire brigade)

15 (SAMU) 18 (Fire brigade / Pompiers)
More detail

In France, both SAMU (15) and the fire brigade (18, Pompiers) respond to medical emergencies. If you're not sure which to call, the EU-wide number 112 connects you to an operator who will route you correctly.

I need an ambulance

Call 15 (SAMU). If you're unsure about your French, the EU-wide 112 works too.

15 (SAMU) 112 (EU-wide)
More detail

112 works anywhere in the EU and is often answered by multilingual operators. If they need to call you back, don't be alarmed by a callback from "0800 112 112" — that's a legitimate emergency services number. A dedicated SMS emergency line, 114, exists for people who are deaf or hard of hearing.

I need the police

Genuine emergency: call 17. Non-urgent theft or damage: book online first (pré-plainte en ligne)

17 (Police)
More detail

For non-urgent theft or property damage reports, pré-plainte en ligne (pre-plainte-en-ligne.gouv.fr) lets you fill in the details online and book a station and time slot in advance, which usually means far less waiting once you get there. A 24-hour chat service (moncommissariat.interieur.gouv.fr) is also available.

I need a pharmacy at night or on a holiday

Call 3237, or search online, to find the on-duty pharmacy (pharmacie de garde) nearest you

3237 (paid line, ~€0.35/min)
More detail

3237 is a phone service that tells you which pharmacy in your area is on duty that day. There's also a web/app version, "MonPharmacien" (monpharmacien-idf.fr), covering Sunday, holiday, and night duty pharmacies across the greater Paris region. An on-duty pharmacy sometimes only serves customers through a night window in the shutter rather than a fully open storefront. Paris also has a small number of pharmacies open 24/7 regardless of the duty roster — for example Pharmacie Centrale (52 Rue du Commerce, 75015) and Pharmacie de la Porte de Montreuil (2 Avenue de la Porte de Montreuil, 75020) — though hours can change, so it's worth a quick call ahead if possible.

My card isn't working

① Check your banking app for a fraud lock, low balance, or an international-use setting
② Always carry a backup card, ideally from a different issuer

More detail

Foreign-issued cards sometimes get flagged and frozen by fraud detection the first time they're used abroad — a quick call to the number on the back (many have a collect-call option for international use) often unlocks it. If you're on Wise or Revolut, double-check you've actually converted funds into euros and that your balance covers the purchase. Traveling with at least two working cards from different providers is cheap insurance.

The metro has stopped

Check line-by-line status in the "Bonjour RATP" app; find an alternate route in "Île-de-France Mobilités"

More detail

Bonjour RATP shows real-time status (normal / delayed / suspended) per line and lets you favorite the lines you use for notifications. For an alternate route across bus, RER, and walking options, Île-de-France Mobilités tends to be more useful. Status is also posted on ratp.fr.

I need to get to the airport fast

Fastest is a rideshare (Uber etc.) or taxi. Public transit can be too slow or infrequent depending on the time and line.

More detail

Full detail on transport options, timing, and current fares for each Paris airport is in the airport transfers guide. If you're genuinely rushed right now, ask your hotel or a café to call you a taxi, or open a rideshare app — that's the most reliable option when timing is tight.
