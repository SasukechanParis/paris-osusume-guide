Paris, plus one more stop

European, UK, and Australian honeymoons average around two weeks — long enough to leave Paris for a few days without feeling rushed (US trips tend to run closer to a week, so this is more of a "if you have the time" page than a "everyone should do this" one). Where you add on depends mostly on the season.

Winter: Strasbourg Spring: The Loire Valley Summer: The Riviera Year-round: London

Winter: Strasbourg & the Alsace Christmas markets

Strasbourg calls itself the Capital of Christmas, and the claim has some history behind it — its market dates to 1570, the oldest in France. By late November the city is running 13 separate Christmas markets across the old town, upwards of 300 stalls, centered on the half-timbered Petite France district that looks the part even without the market stalls. It's a direct TGV from Paris, about 1 hour 46 minutes each way. Book 60+ days out on SNCF Connect and fares typically run €40-90; wait until the last minute and it can jump to €130-180, so this is one to lock in early once you have dates.

Spring: The Loire Valley chateaux

The Loire Valley is the closest of these four to a genuine day trip — just over an hour from Paris Montparnasse to Tours by direct TGV, with local trains connecting on to the châteaux themselves (Amboise is 15-25 minutes from Tours, for example). Chenonceau (built across the river itself), Chambord (the biggest, most castle-shaped of the group), and Amboise are the three worth prioritizing if you're only doing one trip. Late spring is when the formal gardens are at their best, though the châteaux themselves are a reasonable visit in any season — this is the one on this page that doesn't strictly require the right month.

Summer: The French Riviera

Nice, Antibes, and Cannes are a genuinely different trip from Paris — Mediterranean beaches, a slower pace, warm evenings — and a direct TGV from Gare de Lyon gets you there in around 5 hours 30 to 5 hours 45 minutes, with about 8 direct departures a day. That's a full day of travel each way, so this works best as a multi-night stay rather than a quick add-on; if your trip is closer to a week than two, this is the one to skip in favor of somewhere closer. June through August is when the coast is actually warm enough to swim, which is the whole point of going.

Year-round: London

The one on this list that doesn't depend on the season at all. Eurostar runs city-center to city-center in about 2 hours 16 minutes, with 14-18 departures a day — genuinely closer to taking a domestic train than crossing a border, right up until passport control. And that's the part worth planning for: a passport is mandatory (a UK/France national ID card no longer works post-Brexit), and as of February 2026, most visa-exempt visitors — that includes US, Canadian, Australian, and Singaporean passport holders — also need a UK ETA, applied for online, before they travel. It's not something you can sort out at the station; arrive without one and you'll be turned back at the gate. Budget the ETA application into your pre-trip checklist, not your day-of plan.
