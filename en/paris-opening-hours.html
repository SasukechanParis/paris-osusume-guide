Paris on a Sunday or Monday

"Everything in France closes on Sunday" is an overstatement — it depends entirely on what you're looking for. Here's what actually shuts, and when.

The general pattern

Bakeries are usually your safest bet on a Sunday — most stay open through the morning. Independent boutiques are the opposite: many close all day Sunday, and a fair number also close Monday (treating the weekend as Saturday-Sunday-Monday rather than the US five-day-week assumption). Restaurants commonly close one day a week, often Monday, and almost all close for a few hours between lunch and dinner service rather than staying open all afternoon — see the dining guide for restaurant timing specifically.

Museum closing days

Museums each set their own closing day, so check before you plan around one specifically. The two you're most likely to visit: the Louvre closes on Tuesdays, and the Musée d'Orsay closes on Mondays — which conveniently means you can usually do one on a day the other is shut. National museums are also closed on January 1, May 1, and December 25.

One to cross off your list entirely for now: the Centre Pompidou, Paris's major modern art museum, has been fully closed since September 22, 2025 for a large-scale renovation, with reopening not expected until 2030. If an older guidebook or blog post has it on the itinerary, that's why it's out of date.

French public holidays in 2026

On these 11 days, expect most shops, many restaurants, and some museums to close (national museums are closed on three of these — see above): January 1 (New Year's Day), April 6 (Easter Monday), May 1 (Labour Day), May 8 (Victory in Europe Day), May 14 (Ascension Day), May 25 (Whit Monday), July 14 (Bastille Day), August 15 (Assumption Day), November 1 (All Saints' Day), November 11 (Armistice Day), and December 25 (Christmas Day).
